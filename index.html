Predictive torque control of a permanent magnet synchronous motor

How closely can a motor drive follow its torque request when the inverter applies a command one update late? This study compares three controller configurations in simulation.

Read the reportDownload PDFVerify the models

Next step

Compare the delayed and compensated controllers at three speeds and three loads. Recalculate switching activity from the gate signals using the same counting rule at each point.

For discussion

Select a representative operating point for a comparison at similar switching frequency. A supervised bench baseline is still to be agreed.

Further findings

Current waveform

At 1,000 rpm and 1 N·m, phase-current distortion measured 30.3% for A2 and 12.2% for A3. This is the strength of harmonics 2–50 relative to the fundamental, averaged over all three phases.

Simulated phase currents, 0.660–0.900 s, end excluded. Current data

Magnet-flux setting

At 1,000 rpm and 1 N·m, A3 drew 1.275 A of effective phase current with the correct magnet-flux setting. It drew 2.347 A with the setting 20% low and 2.452 A with it 20% high. The motor and load stayed the same.

Root mean square across the three simulated phase currents, 0.65–0.90 s. Flux-sweep data

First bench run

At one agreed speed and load, use the existing rig controller to record speed, phase currents and faults on the same clock. This supervised baseline would show whether the rig can capture the signals needed to compare controllers.
